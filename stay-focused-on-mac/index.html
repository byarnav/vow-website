How to stay focused on your Mac

Last updated: November 14, 2026

To stay focused on a Mac or MacBook, turn on Do Not Disturb, switch off the notifications you don’t need, work in one full-screen window, and keep your phone in another room. For your hardest work, like studying for an exam, turn the internet off until a timer ends, so there’s nothing to check.

Why it’s so hard to focus on a Mac

Your Mac is where you work, and also where every distraction lives. In Gloria Mark’s studies, people stayed on one screen for about 47 seconds on average before switching.[1] Every alert, tab and “quick check” pulls you away. The fix is to remove as many of them as you can before you start.

7 steps to stay focused on your Mac

  1. Turn on Do Not Disturb. Click Control Center in the menu bar, click Focus, then Do Not Disturb. To pick which people and apps can still reach you, open System Settings and click Focus.
  2. Turn off notifications you don’t need. Open System Settings, click Notifications, click an app, and turn off Allow notifications. Start with chat, social and news apps.
  3. Use one window, full screen. Click the green button at the top left of a window. Everything else disappears.
  4. Close the tabs you aren’t using. Bookmark them if you’re scared to lose them.
  5. Put your phone in another room. In one study, just having a phone on the desk, even switched off, lowered scores on thinking tests.[2]
  6. Block the sites that pull you in. Screen Time can block Instagram, YouTube, TikTok and others for free. See how to block websites on a Mac.
  7. Go fully offline for your hardest work. Pick a time and turn the internet off until it ends. Vow does this from 5 minutes to 24 hours, and quitting or restarting won’t end it early.

For students: SAT, JEE, NEET, UPSC and finals

When you’re studying for a big exam, “just one video” is the biggest thief of time. It costs focus too. In one study, students who multitasked on a laptop during a lecture scored lower on a test, and so did students sitting near them.[3]

  • Download practice papers, notes and recorded lectures before you start.
  • Study in blocks, like 2 hours, then take a break.
  • Use a schedule so the block starts on its own. In Vow, click Schedules, then Add Schedule, for example every day at 7:00 for 2 hours.
  • Write down anything you need to look up, and search it in your break.

Doesn’t Focus mode do this already?

No. Focus mode hides notifications, but the internet stays on. YouTube, Instagram and the news are still one click away. Focus mode helps with interruptions from others. A blocker helps with interruptions you start yourself.
