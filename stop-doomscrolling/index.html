How to stop doomscrolling on your Mac

Last updated: November 14, 2026

Doomscrolling is scrolling news or endless feeds long after it stops helping you. Feeds are built to have no end, so stopping by willpower alone is hard. Studies found that people who cut back felt better. The most reliable fix is to make the feed unreachable for a set time, and on a Mac, Vow does that by turning off your internet until a timer ends.

What doomscrolling is, and why it’s hard to stop

Doomscrolling means scrolling bad news or endless feeds long after it stops helping you. Most feeds have no bottom. Videos play the next one on their own. There’s never a natural place to stop, so you stop when you’re tired, not when you’re done.

It isn’t harmless. In surveys, people who doomscroll more also report more stress and anxiety and lower well-being.[1] Those surveys show a link, not the cause, but most people know the feeling: an hour gone, and you feel worse than before.

It’s not only the news

The same loop happens with lots of things people search for help with:

  • YouTube: one video for a break, then autoplay and the sidebar take the evening.
  • Reddit and X: one more thread, one more reply, one more refresh.
  • Short videos: Reels, Shorts and TikTok in the browser are built to keep you swiping.
  • Constant checking: email, messages, scores and prices, many times an hour. Gloria Mark’s studies found people stayed on one screen for about 47 seconds before switching.[2]

If you’ve ever typed “internet addiction” or “YouTube addiction” into a search bar, the steps below are for you.

What happened when people cut back

  • Less social media, less loneliness. 143 students limited Facebook, Instagram and Snapchat to 10 minutes each a day for three weeks. They felt less lonely and less depressed than students who didn’t, especially those who started out more depressed.[3]
  • A month off, and people kept using it less. In a large study, people who turned off Facebook for four weeks spent more time offline, felt a bit happier, and used Facebook less even after they could go back.[4]
  • Two weeks offline, better attention. 467 adults blocked the internet on their phones for two weeks. 91% got better on at least one of attention, mental health or well-being.[5]

These studies didn’t test Vow. They show that less scrolling tends to help. Vow is one way to get there on a Mac.

Steps that work on a Mac

  1. Find your scroll hour. For most people it’s late at night, right after work, or when a task gets hard. Write it down.
  2. Make it harder to start. Log out of the sites you scroll. Remove them from your bookmarks and your browser’s start page.
  3. Try Screen Time for small limits. In System Settings, click Screen Time, then App & Website Activity to see where your time goes, and App Limits to set a daily limit. It’s free. The catch: if you set the passcode yourself, it takes seconds to get around.
  4. Make your scroll hour offline. Set Vow to turn off your Mac’s internet at that time every day. Open Vow, click Schedules, then Add Schedule. For example: every day at 22:30 for 8 hours. Quitting Vow or restarting your Mac won’t end it early.
  5. Charge your phone outside the bedroom. Vow only works on your Mac. If your phone is next to the bed, the scrolling just moves.
  6. Have something ready to do instead. A book, a downloaded podcast, a show saved for offline. Saying no is easier when you know what you’re saying yes to.

Studying for a big exam?

Students preparing for the SAT, JEE, NEET, UPSC (IAS) or finals often lose more hours to “just one video” than to anything else. It costs more than time. In one study, students who multitasked on a laptop during a lecture scored lower on a test afterwards, and so did students who could see their screens.[6]

A simple plan: download your practice papers, notes and recorded lectures first, then go offline for one study block, like 2 hours. Use a Vow schedule so it starts at the same time every day, even when you don’t feel like it.

When it feels like more than a habit

Sometimes scrolling is a habit. Sometimes it’s something you can’t stop even when it’s hurting your sleep, money, studies or relationships: online gambling or betting, adult sites, or gaming. The World Health Organization lists gaming disorder as a health condition.[7] If this sounds like you, you’re not alone, and it isn’t a lack of willpower.

Blocking can help by putting a gap between the urge and the action. With Vow, the internet on your Mac stays off until your timer ends, and asking to unlock early means waiting 15 more minutes first. Urges often fade in that time. But blocking is only one part. Real support helps more:

  • Gambling, US: National Problem Gambling Helpline, call or text 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738), or chat online. Free and open all day, every day.[8]
  • Gambling, UK: National Gambling Helpline, 0808 8020 133. Free, every day, all day.[9]
  • India: Tele MANAS, the government’s free mental health helpline, 14416 or 1-800-891-4416, day and night.[10]
  • Anywhere: talk to a doctor or a counselor you trust. If you ever feel unsafe, call your local emergency number.

Vow only covers your Mac. If gambling is the problem, also ask your bank about blocking gambling payments and look up self-exclusion in your country.

Questions

How do I stop doomscrolling at night?

Make your late-night hours offline. Charge your phone outside the bedroom, and set your Mac to go offline at the same time every night. In Vow, click Schedules, then Add Schedule, and pick a time like 22:30 for 8 hours.

Can I block YouTube and Reddit on my Mac?

Yes. To block only a few sites, use Screen Time or a website blocker like SelfControl. To stop jumping from one site to the next, Vow turns off your Mac’s whole internet until your timer ends, so YouTube, Reddit and everything else are off at once.

Is doomscrolling an addiction?

Doomscrolling is a habit, not a diagnosis. But for some people, scrolling, gaming, gambling or adult sites become hard to control. If it’s hurting your life, talk to a doctor or counselor. A blocker can help, but it isn’t treatment.

Does Vow block my phone too?

No. Vow works on your Mac only. Keep your phone in another room, or set limits on it with Screen Time.

Can students use Vow while studying for exams?

Yes. Download your notes, practice papers and recorded lectures first, then go offline for a study block. Everything saved on your Mac keeps working while the internet is off.
