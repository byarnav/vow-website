Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and Reddit addiction: how to stop on your Mac

Last updated: November 14, 2026

If you open Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Reddit or X without thinking and can’t stop even when you want to, you’re not alone. Apps are built to keep you scrolling. Studies found that people who cut back felt less lonely and less depressed. This guide covers the signs, what research shows, and simple steps that work on a Mac.

Signs it’s more than “a lot of use”

Researchers who built one of the first social media addiction scales looked for six signs.[1] Ask yourself how often these are true:

  • You think about the app a lot, even when you’re not on it.
  • You need more and more time on it to feel the same.
  • You use it to forget your problems or escape bad feelings.
  • You’ve tried to cut down and couldn’t.
  • You feel restless or upset when you can’t use it.
  • It’s hurting your work, studies, sleep or relationships.

This isn’t a diagnosis. But if several sound like you, it’s worth acting.

Why Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and Reddit are so hard to stop

  • Instagram: Reels and Explore never run out, and other people’s best moments make your normal day look worse.
  • TikTok: each video is short, so “one more” always feels small.
  • YouTube: autoplay and the sidebar choose the next video for you.
  • Reddit and X (Twitter): one more thread, one more reply, one more refresh, often full of bad news.

The comparison part is real. When Facebook arrived at US colleges, students’ mental health got worse, and researchers linked it to comparing themselves with others.[2] People who scroll bad news a lot also report more stress and lower well-being.[6]

What happened when people cut back

  • Three weeks, 10 minutes a day: students who limited Facebook, Instagram and Snapchat to 10 minutes each a day felt less lonely and less depressed.[3]
  • Four weeks off: people who turned off Facebook spent more time offline, felt a bit happier, and used it less even afterwards.[4]
  • Two weeks without phone internet: 91% of 467 adults got better in attention, mental health or well-being.[5]

How to stop on your Mac or MacBook

  1. Find your scroll hours. Late at night, after work, or when a task gets hard. Write them down.
  2. Log out and remove shortcuts. Sign out of Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Reddit and X in your browser. Remove them from bookmarks.
  3. Block the worst sites. Screen Time can block them for free. Here are the steps and exact addresses: how to block websites on a Mac.
  4. Make your scroll hours offline. If you block one app and just open another, turn off the whole internet instead. In Vow, click Schedules, then Add Schedule, for example every night at 22:30 for 8 hours. Quitting Vow or restarting won’t end it early.
  5. Charge your phone outside the bedroom. Vow works on your Mac only. If your phone is next to you, the scrolling just moves.
  6. Have something ready instead. A book, a downloaded podcast, a walk, a friend to call.

When you can’t stop: gambling, gaming and adult sites

Sometimes it’s not social media but betting sites, online games or adult sites. The World Health Organization lists gaming disorder as a health condition.[7] If you can’t stop even though it’s hurting you, it isn’t just weak willpower, and help works.

  • Gambling, US: National Problem Gambling Helpline, call or text 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738), or chat online, any time.[8]
  • Gambling, UK: National Gambling Helpline, 0808 8020 133, free, any time.[9]
  • India: Tele MANAS, the government’s free mental health helpline, 14416 or 1-800-891-4416, day and night.[10]
  • Adult sites: see how to block adult sites on a Mac, and talk to a doctor or counselor.
  • If you ever feel unsafe, call your local emergency number.

Questions

How do I stop Instagram addiction on my Mac?

Block instagram.com with Screen Time, sign out in your browser, and make your worst hours offline. If you block Instagram and open TikTok or YouTube instead, use Vow to turn off the whole internet until a timer ends.

How do I stop TikTok and YouTube addiction?

Find the hours you scroll most and make them offline. Block the sites on your Mac, keep your phone in another room, and have something else ready to do.

Is internet addiction real?

“Internet addiction” isn’t an official diagnosis, but gaming disorder and gambling disorder are. Many people struggle to control their social media use. If it’s hurting your life, talk to a doctor or counselor.
