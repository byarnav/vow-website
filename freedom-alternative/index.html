Vow vs Freedom: a one-time-purchase Freedom alternative for Mac

Last updated: November 5, 2026

Freedom blocks the websites and apps on your lists and syncs across your devices. Vow turns off your Mac's whole internet connection until a timer ends, with nothing to set up. Pick Freedom to block some sites on several devices. Pick Vow to take your Mac fully offline for a while, for $9.99 once.

The short verdict

Choose Freedom if…

  • You want one block across your Mac, Windows PC, iPhone, iPad, Android or Chromebook.
  • You want to block specific sites and apps and keep the rest of the internet.
  • You like managing sessions from a cloud dashboard.

Choose Vow if…

  • You want everything quiet, including Discord, Slack, Telegram, WhatsApp and Mail, without building lists.
  • You use a work or commercial VPN, or rely on localhost and local dev tools.
  • You'd rather pay once than subscribe, and you don't want an account.

How blocking works differently

Freedom blocks what's on your lists. On a Mac it routes web traffic through a local system proxy (or a Non-Proxy browser filter) and adds an app blocker. That's flexible: you can block social media and keep everything else. But a system proxy can conflict with work and commercial VPNs, and local development servers need proxy bypass rules for localhost and 127.0.0.1.

Vow doesn't filter anything. It turns off your Mac's network connections using built-in macOS tools, so every browser, app and terminal tool goes offline at once. There are no lists to maintain and no proxy to conflict with your VPN. localhost keeps working because the loopback interface stays up.

Staying locked

In Freedom's standard mode, you can cancel a session instantly. Locked Mode, part of Freedom Premium, keeps a session running until the timer ends. Stopping Freedom from being quit requires Locked Mode together with its "Block Activity Monitor" option.

Vow has no instant cancel. You can request an early unlock, and your Mac stays offline for 15 more minutes first. A system helper holds the timer, so quitting Vow or restarting your Mac changes nothing.

Switching from Freedom to Vow

  • You don't need to rebuild your lists. Vow has none: the whole internet goes off.
  • Recreate recurring sessions as Vow schedules, for example weekdays at 9:00 for 2 hours.
  • Start with a short session, say 30 minutes, to see how working fully offline feels. You get 3 full sessions free.
  • Keep Freedom for your other devices if you use it on your phone. The two don't need to replace each other.

Questions

Is Vow cheaper than Freedom?

Yes, for a Mac. Vow is $9.99 once with all 1.x updates included, for up to 2 personal Macs. Freedom is $8.99 a month, $39.99 a year, or $99.50 for lifetime, and it covers more types of devices.

Can Vow block only certain websites, like Freedom?

No. Vow turns off your Mac's whole internet connection, so there are no lists. If you want to block some sites and keep the rest, Freedom or Cold Turkey is the better fit.

Does Vow work on iPhone or Windows?

No. Vow is a Mac app and requires macOS 26 or later on Apple Silicon.

Will Vow conflict with my VPN the way a proxy can?

No. Vow never touches proxies, /etc/hosts or DNS settings. During a session your VPN is offline along with everything else, and when the session ends your network is restored exactly as it was.
