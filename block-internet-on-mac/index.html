How to block the internet on your Mac for a set time

Last updated: November 5, 2026

The quickest way is to turn off Wi-Fi, but it takes one click to undo. For a block that holds, you need something that turns off every connection and keeps it off until a timer ends, even if you quit apps or restart. This guide covers the built-in macOS options first, then how Vow does it.

The quick answer

  • For a quick break: turn off Wi-Fi in Control Center. It's instant, and it's one click to undo.
  • For a block that holds: use an app that takes your whole Mac offline on a timer and doesn't let you cancel on impulse. Vow does this for anywhere from 5 minutes to 24 hours, and quitting the app or restarting your Mac won't bring the internet back.

Built-in ways to turn off the internet on a Mac

macOS has no "go offline until 5 pm" switch, but you can get part of the way with what's already on your Mac.

1. Turn off Wi-Fi

Click the Wi-Fi icon in the menu bar or Control Center and switch it off, or go to System Settings › Wi-Fi. It's free and instant. The catch: it's one click to turn back on, there's no timer, and it only stops Wi-Fi. An Ethernet cable, a USB network adapter or an iPhone connected by USB can still get you online.

2. Use Terminal

If you're comfortable with the command line, networksetup -setairportpower en0 off turns Wi-Fi off, and the same command with on turns it back on. Wi-Fi is usually en0 on a Mac laptop; run networksetup -listallhardwareports to check yours. It has the same weakness as the Wi-Fi switch: one command undoes it, and nothing turns it back on for you.

3. Screen Time

Screen Time (System Settings › Screen Time) can limit apps with Downtime and App Limits, and its Content & Privacy settings can restrict websites. It was designed for parents managing a child's Mac. When you set the passcode yourself, you can switch it off in seconds, and it limits apps and sites by list rather than turning off the network.

4. Focus modes

Focus modes silence notifications, but your internet stays on. They're a good companion to a block, not a block.

5. Your router

Many routers can pause the internet on a schedule. That's hard to undo from your Mac, but it affects everyone at home, and an iPhone hotspot gets around it immediately.

Why most blocks don't hold

Block YouTube, and you open Netflix. Block WhatsApp, and you move to Telegram. Close Safari, and you open Chrome. Lists of blocked sites invite you to find the gap, and a block you can undo in one click is only as strong as your willpower in that moment.

A block that actually holds needs three things:

  1. It covers every connection: Wi-Fi, Ethernet, USB adapters and hotspots, for every app and browser.
  2. It can't be cancelled on impulse, but it still has a way out for a real emergency.
  3. It survives you quitting the app or restarting your Mac.

Questions

Can I turn off the internet on my Mac without turning off Bluetooth?

Yes. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are separate, so turning off Wi-Fi leaves Bluetooth on. Vow also leaves Bluetooth alone, so your AirPods, mouse, keyboard and Apple Watch keep working during a session.

Does turning off Wi-Fi also block Ethernet or an iPhone hotspot?

No. Turning off Wi-Fi only stops Wi-Fi. A plugged-in Ethernet cable, a USB network adapter or an iPhone connected by USB can still get you online. Vow turns off all of them.

Can I schedule my Mac's internet to turn off at night?

macOS has no built-in way to turn off the network at a set time. Screen Time's Downtime limits apps, but it doesn't take your Mac offline. With Vow, you can add a schedule, such as every weekday at 21:00 for 2 hours, and the session starts on its own.

Will localhost and local AI models still work while I'm offline?

Yes, with Vow. The loopback interface stays up, so localhost, local dev servers, local containers via localhost ports, and local AI models such as Ollama keep working.
